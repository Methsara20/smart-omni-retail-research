01 / RESEARCH FOUNDATIONS

Research Domain Overview

Multi-store fashion retail brings together product availability, promotion targeting, fitting decisions, and in-store customer assistance. The paper investigates how AI can connect these operational and customer-facing decisions through shared services and data.

Demand forecasting and stock-flow optimisation address retail operations; personalised marketing, virtual fitting, brand-specific sizing, and movement-based assistance address the customer experience. Their integration is the research focus. The reported model results do not establish measured business improvements or production readiness.

Source: research paper, Abstract and Section I.

02 / RELATED RESEARCH

Literature Survey

Five themes summarise Section II of the paper. Citation numbers retain the paper's bibliography; literature methods are distinguished from the implemented research system.

THEME 01

Retail Forecasting and Inventory Intelligence

Retail forecasting research supports inventory planning and operational decisions. The paper identifies XGBoost as an approach for modelling non-linear sales patterns and sparse features. Forecasting therefore supplies demand estimates that can inform replenishment and redistribution. [3] [22] [23]

Connection to this research

The retail problem described in Section I extends beyond prediction: stock decisions must account for availability across stores. Our research links demand forecasts to inventory-health analysis and risk-aware stock-transfer decisions; Section V notes prepared or simulated data and simplified logistics assumptions.

THEME 02

Personalised Marketing

CRM, RFM analysis and Customer Lifetime Value provide foundations for customer segmentation. The literature also discusses uplift modelling for promotion targeting. These approaches help distinguish customer groups and support targeted campaign decisions. [5] [6] [21]

Connection to this research

Section I identifies broad segmentation and limited stock-aware targeting as retail challenges. Our research uses RFM features and XGBoost to predict promotion-redemption likelihood and connect promotions to product availability. It does not report implementing uplift modelling; campaign and customer data quality remain limitations.

THEME 03

Virtual Try-On and Fashion Recommendation

VITON addresses virtual fitting through garment overlays; characteristic-preserving alignment aims to retain garment textures, logos and patterns. Visual recommendation and computer-vision research provide further context for intelligent apparel selection. [4] [11] [13] [15]

Connection to this research

Section I identifies separation between measurement estimation and garment visualisation as a research concern. Our approach combines measurement estimation, brand-specific sizing, Vertex AI visualisation and a product-grounded stylist. Section V retains limitations from inaccurate measurements, underrepresented brands and difficult images.

THEME 04

Customer Behaviour Intelligence

Location-based research provides background for movement analysis and customer-assistance decisions. Random Forest offers a classification foundation for behavioural datasets. The paper applies this direction to mobile-GPS trajectories and browsing/transiting classification. [7] [10]

Connection to this research

Our research uses GPS-derived movement features to classify browsing and transiting and trigger assistance alerts.

Dedicated indoor-tracking hardware and manual monitoring can constrain retail adoption (Section I). Mobile GPS supports a practical alternative, while Section V identifies indoor accuracy, battery usage and the need for real-world movement validation as limitations.

THEME 05

Explainable and Generative AI

SHAP and LIME are reviewed as explanation methods. Language and multimodal models support content generation and conversational recommendations. [8] [9] [16] [17] [18] [19]

Connection to this research

The implemented system uses Gemini explanations and a RAG stylist. SHAP and LIME are related literature, not reported implementations.

Identified Research Gap

The gap concerns limited integration among the particular capabilities investigated, rather than an absence of all integrated retail platforms.

FRAGMENTED RETAIL INTELLIGENCE

  • Independent forecasting systems
  • Separate promotion decisions
  • Isolated virtual fitting tools
  • Limited behavioural integration
  • Disconnected data and decisions
INTEGRATED SMART OMNI-RETAIL RESEARCH

A shared foundation for intelligence.

  • Shared data infrastructure
  • Connected intelligent functions
  • Stock-aware marketing
  • Integrated customer assistance
  • Coordinated retail decision support

The research gap centres on coordinating operational predictions and customer-facing decisions through shared information. Forecasting informs stock availability; promotion intelligence uses that availability; measurement-aware fitting supports product selection; movement analysis supports staff assistance. The study investigates these relationships within one architecture, with real-world effectiveness still requiring validation.

04 / RESEARCH QUESTION

Research Problem

Across multiple stores, demand, stock availability, customer preferences, fitting needs, and movement information must be coordinated. Independent tools can leave these decisions disconnected.

How can multiple AI-driven retail functions be integrated through a shared architecture to support coordinated inventory decisions, personalised customer experiences, and timely in-store assistance?

Website-friendly synthesis of Sections I and III.A; not a verbatim quotation from the paper.

Inventory and stock flow

Demand and stock can be imbalanced between stores. Forecasts need to support explainable transfer decisions while accounting for source-store risk and logistics constraints.

Personalised marketing

Broad segmentation can miss customer-specific offer relevance. Promotion decisions need customer and campaign evidence together with available stock.

Virtual try-on and sizing

Garment visualisation and measurement-based fit can be disconnected. Customers need brand-specific sizing and virtual fitting that account for their supplied measurements.

Customer behaviour and assistance

Retail staff need timely context about customer movement and assistance needs. Location-based classification must account for indoor accuracy and practical monitoring constraints.

Component problems synthesised from Sections I, III.B–E and V.

  1. 01

  2. 02

06 / THE RESEARCH PROCESS

Research Methodology

The six-stage process in Fig. 2 moves from requirements to evaluation, with iterative improvement across development stages.

Research design and evaluation

The paper reports a development-and-evaluation process combining prepared or simulated retail records with component-specific sales, customer/campaign, measurement/fit and movement inputs. Preprocessing and feature engineering precede model development: lag/rolling-demand features support XGBoost regression, RFM features support redemption classification, and measurement and trajectory inputs support Random Forest models.

Body-measurement model tuning uses GridSearchCV with three-fold cross-validation. Regression is evaluated using MAE, RMSE and R²; classifiers use accuracy and component-specific precision, recall and F1. Functional testing and integration are described in Section III.A, while Section V limits conclusions from simulated data and operational constraints.

Read the four component workflows and review the evaluation results. Full-platform deployment and real-world testing remain future research.

08 / ONE INTERCONNECTED SYSTEM

Integrated System Architecture

Customers, retail staff, and administrators access a Flutter Web interface. FastAPI provides shared services, business logic, and model serving; Firebase Authentication supports authentication in the application layer.

Shared identifiers connect the research functions.

Product, customer, and transaction identifiers support information exchange. Common product and inventory records inform stock-aware marketing, customer profiles support measurement prediction, and behavioural records support assistance requests and staff notifications.

This is the architecture of the research product. The academic website remains HTML and CSS only.

09 / INTELLIGENT FUNCTIONS IN DETAIL

Four Intelligent Research Components

Each function contributes a distinct method to the common platform. Reported model metrics describe the paper's evaluation, rather than proven business outcomes.

01
RESEARCH FUNCTION 01

Smart Inventory & Stock Flow Optimisation

Predict future product demand and support stock movement between retail locations.

Methodology & AI techniques

XGBoost regression uses historical sales, lag features, seven-day rolling averages, product attributes, store information, promotions, weather, and calendar variables for daily and monthly demand forecasting.

Key features

  • Inventory-health analysis, days-on-hand, shortage, reorder and overstock detection.
  • Candidate source-store ranking using available stock, forecast demand, safety stock, distance, logistics cost and shortage risk.
  • Risk and confidence indicators, Gemini-based natural-language explanations, and a review, approve/reject/cancel, execute workflow.

Integration role

FastAPI services and MongoDB connect product, store, inventory, sales, forecast, recommendation, transaction and movement-history records. Transfer execution updates inventory and movement history.

Research limitations

Primarily simulated or prepared retail data and simplified logistics assumptions limit deployment conclusions. Human approval is required before transfer execution.

02
RESEARCH FUNCTION 02

Personalised Marketing Intelligence

Predict customer promotion-redemption likelihood and inform personalised campaign decisions.

Methodology & AI techniques

Recency, Frequency, and Monetary (RFM) features, together with customer, product and promotion attributes, feed an XGBoost classifier. Predicted redemption probability supports segmentation and scheduling.

Key features

  • Customer segmentation and promotion scheduling informed by predicted redemption likelihood.
  • Generative promotional assets using the paper-described Gemini Imagen 3 API.
  • Gemini 1.5 Flash explanations, CSV upload and cleaning, and customer-intelligence analysis.

Integration role

MongoDB shares product availability and overstock information with inventory intelligence so that campaign decisions account for stock.

Research limitations

Performance depends on complete, good-quality customer and campaign data. Generated assets and explanations depend on the information supplied to the generative model.

03
RESEARCH FUNCTION 03

Virtual Try-On & Size Recommendation

Combine body-measurement estimation, brand-specific sizing, garment visualisation and product-grounded styling.

Methodology & AI techniques

A Random Forest Regressor, tuned with GridSearchCV and three-fold cross-validation, predicts shoulder width, waist circumference and leg length from height, weight, gender and BMI. A 100-tree Random Forest Classifier maps standard size to brand-specific size.

Key features

  • Vertex AI virtual-try-on-001 processes a customer photograph and selected garment image.
  • Updated stored height or weight triggers measurement recalculation; MongoDB holds profiles and predicted measurements.
  • The RAG stylist embeds product metadata, descriptions and reviews with HuggingFace all-MiniLM-L6-v2. ChromaDB stores embeddings, and LangChain retrieves relevant in-stock products.

Integration role

The customer-facing Flutter interface combines returned measurements, brand-size recommendations and garment images. Retrieval supplies product context for the language model; RAG is not the size-prediction algorithm.

Research limitations

Complex poses or obscured images may cause visual artefacts. Accuracy depends on user-provided height and weight, represented brands, and complete indexed product metadata.

04
RESEARCH FUNCTION 04

Customer Behaviour Tracking & Staff Assistance

Classify customer movement and support timely in-store staff assistance.

Methodology & AI techniques

Mobile GPS coordinates sent every three seconds are mapped to predefined store zones. Haversine distance, velocity and direction-change features feed a 100-tree Random Forest Classifier for Browsing/Transiting classification; a speed-rule fallback is available when the model is unavailable.

Key features

  • Dwell-time and zone-transition analysis supports assistance alerts.
  • Continuous browsing for at least 30 seconds, or Zone A to Zone B to Zone A within 60 seconds, can generate an alert.
  • Staff monitoring displays locations and assistance requests; customers can request manual SOS assistance.

Integration role

FastAPI monitoring services and MongoDB share zones, monitoring records, assistance requests and staff notifications. The customer application transmits locations; the staff dashboard polls the backend.

Research limitations

Indoor GPS accuracy can degrade; continuous background tracking can affect battery life. The store app must remain active in the background, and real-world movement data may require classifier retraining.

11 / CONTRIBUTION & BOUNDARIES

Research Contributions & Limitations

Operational and customer intelligence together

The integrated contribution is a common services and retail-data architecture that connects four intelligent functions.

  • Forecasting linked to explainable, risk-aware stock-transfer decisions and execution support.
  • Redemption prediction linked to promotional assets and stock availability.
  • Measurement regression, brand-size classification, generative try-on and a product-grounded conversational stylist.
  • Mobile-GPS movement classification linked to automated behavioural assistance alerts.

Scope of the evidence

  • Partially simulated or prepared datasets require real-world validation.
  • Simplified logistics assumptions and human approval constrain automated stock transfer.
  • Personalisation depends on complete customer, campaign and product data.
  • Virtual try-on may produce image artefacts; measurement inputs and brand coverage affect sizing.
  • Indoor GPS accuracy and background battery usage limit tracking.
  • The movement classifier may require retraining with real-world data; the store application must remain active in the background.

The system is not presented as fully production-validated.

Source: Section V.A–D.
